# M20.5 — Arabic language tools: review

## `content/roots.json` — verification trail

Standing rule 1 ("never invent Arabic") applies in full to a root/pattern table — a wrong root is exactly the kind of "real-looking but wrong" content that rule exists to prevent. Every entry below was selected only if it met two bars: (a) it's a genuine, defensible Arabic root fact, and (b) **both** lexemes already exist, verbatim, in `content/lexemes.json` — nothing was invented to fill out a pair.

Most entries are standard, textbook-level MSA morphology (Form I verb ↔ derived noun patterns) that any reference grammar or dictionary (Hans Wehr, standard pedagogical texts) confirms without controversy. For the handful of entries checked directly against an external source during this milestone — either because they were less certain, or because getting them wrong would be the most misleading kind of error (a plausible-looking but false pattern) — the actual searches and sources:

- **مدينة (madīnah, "city") — root question, resolved as *not* a clean-fit case, excluded from `roots.json`.** I considered pairing it with a root-ن family entry but the root (م-د-ن) turned out to be denominal and borrowed from Aramaic, not a productive MSA root with other lexemes.json members — no genuine pair exists in the current vocabulary, so it's simply not in the file. [مدينة – Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D9%85%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%86%D8%A9)
- **شمس (shams, "sun") — considered and excluded** for the same reason (a real root ش-م-س, but no second lexemes.json member shares it): [shams – Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/shams), [شمس – Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D8%B4%D9%85%D8%B3)
- **بيت (bayt, "house") — verified root ب-ي-ت**, considered and ultimately excluded (no second lexemes.json member — `lex:hom-11` "بَيْتٌ" is a case-ending variant of the same word, not a distinct root-mate): [بيت – Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%AA)
- **السَّبْت (as-sabt, "Saturday") vs. سَبْعَة (sabʿah, "seven") — checked and *rejected* as a pair.** These look similar (sabt / sabʿah) and it would have been an easy, plausible-sounding mistake to include them as a root match. They are not: *as-sabt* comes from a root meaning "to rest" (cognate with Hebrew Shabbat), while *sabʿah* is an unrelated numeral root. Excluded from `roots.json` specifically because it's the kind of error that "sounds right" — precisely what standing rule 1 is for. [Where do the Arabic names of weekdays come from? – Transparent Language](https://blogs.transparent.com/arabic/where-do-the-arabic-names-of-weekdays-come-from/), [سبت – Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D8%B3%D8%A8%D8%AA)
- **The weekday-from-number family** (وَاحِد/الْأَحَد, اِثْنَان/الِاثْنَيْن, ثَلَاثَة/الثُّلَاثَاء, أَرْبَعَة/الْأَرْبِعَاء, خَمْسَة/الْخَمِيس) — confirmed as a well-documented pattern (5 of 7 Arabic weekday names derive directly from the ordinal numbers 1–5; Friday derives separately from "gather" جمع, Saturday from "rest" as noted above, neither of which pairs with a number in the current lexeme set) before including all five pairs: [Days Of The Week In Arabic – Mezzoguild](https://www.mezzoguild.com/learn/arabic/vocabulary/days-of-the-week/), [Where do the Arabic names of weekdays come from? – Transparent Language](https://blogs.transparent.com/arabic/where-do-the-arabic-names-of-weekdays-come-from/)

**What was deliberately left out**, beyond the two excluded near-misses above: colors (أَحْمَر, أَزْرَق, …) share a common *pattern* (أفعل, the "elative" color/defect template) but not a common *root* with each other or with anything else in `content/lexemes.json` — pattern-family grouping is a different, not-yet-built feature (see §7/§8 of the scope doc); gender pairs already obviously related by the ة ending alone (كَبِير/كَبِيرَة, طَالِب/طَالِبَة, etc.) were excluded as low pedagogical value — too transparently related to make a useful "confusable" quiz distractor; loanwords with no productive Arabic root (بُرْتُقَالِيّ "orange" ← Portugal, شَاي "tea", مُوز "banana", تِين "fig", تُوت "berry") were not assigned a root at all.

**Result: 11 verified root clusters, 24 lexeme entries total** — a deliberately small, fully-checked starting set per the scope doc's own recommendation, meant to grow incrementally (with the same discipline) as the vocabulary grows in M21.

## `normalizeArabicInput()` and the derivation functions

Both are mechanical Unicode transformations, not linguistic claims, so they carry none of the "never invent Arabic" risk `roots.json` does:

- `normalizeArabicInput()` maps the alef variants (أ إ آ ا) to bare alef and tāʾ marbūṭa/hāʾ (ة ه) together for *comparison only* — the learner's own typed input is never rewritten on screen, matching the scope doc's invariant.
- `deriveReducedForm()`/`deriveUnvowelledForm()` generalize the existing `stripArabicDiacritics()` (in use since Milestone 1) to strip the full ḥarakāt/tanwīn/sukūn/shaddah range, registered into `tools/build-content.js --lint` as an advisory cross-check against `content/texts.json`'s hand-authored `reduced`/`unvowelled` fields — a mismatch is flagged for human review, never auto-corrected.

Run against the real, current `content/texts.json`: **zero mismatches** — the hand-authored `reduced`/`unvowelled` fields are already mechanically consistent with their `vowelled` source. See the acceptance-criteria verification below for the deliberately-broken-example check.
